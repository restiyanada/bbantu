Changelog — v1.2 → v1.3

NEW $0 Infrastructure Track

This revision optimizes the notification layer for a zero-cost MVP with no custom domain, no paid email provider, and no customer accounts.
  • Email events leanified (§17): Reduced from 9 events to 4 core emails. Skipped: payment verified as standalone, shipped, picked up, completed. The order page link is the source of truth for all status confirmations.
  • Order confirmation timing changed (§7.2, §17): The first customer email is now sent after admin verifies payment — not at order submission. This combines "order confirmed" and "payment verified" into a single P1 email. The customer receives their order page URL on the checkout success screen and via push notification opt-in.
  • Push notifications added (§17a): Web Push introduced as a $0 backup channel for P0/P1 events. No customer account required. Covers payment rejected, balance due, and ready for fulfilment.
  • Resend-only provider (§24): Removed multi-provider complexity. Resend free tier is the sole email provider for MVP. No custom domain required; sender uses @resend.dev.
  • Link security reinforced (§16, §27): Order access tokens are high-entropy (32+ bytes), rate-limited, and validated against phone+email cross-check for recovery.
  • WhatsApp manual fallback acknowledged (§17a): Staff can export phone numbers and send template messages for urgent payment chasing when email/push fail.
  • Cap management specified (§24): 100/day Resend hard limit. P0 (rejected, balance due) never deferred. P1 (order confirmed, ready) deferred to next day if cap hit. Balance-due batches >80 are spread across 2 days.

1. Executive Summary

The system is a custom web application for managing two sales modes: batch-based pre-orders and ready stock. It is intentionally not a marketplace. The business controls when a pre-order opens/closes, collects customer orders, procures from suppliers, receives stock, and fulfils orders through booth pickup or shipping.

Core principle: keep the customer experience simple while giving the small internal team strong operational control over orders, payment verification, inventory, fulfilment, shipping, pickup and audit history.

The target operating model is a small business with approximately three staff and one physical fulfilment location. Customers are guests and do not need accounts.

2. Product Vision & Goals

Create a focused order and fulfilment system with enough automation to eliminate spreadsheet-style operational work, without introducing marketplace complexity.

Goals

  • Run time-bound pre-order batches.
  • Sell physically available ready stock.
  • Track customer commitments separately from physical inventory.
  • Support full payment and DP/partial payment.
  • Support manual bank-transfer verification.
  • Support booth pickup using QR scanning from a phone camera.
  • Support JNE shipping and separate shipping charges.
  • Generate printer-friendly shipping stickers.
  • Provide tracking numbers and shipment status.
  • Send useful transactional emails without requiring immediate delivery for every event.
  • Maintain a complete audit log.
  • Provide an order timeline.
  • Work as a responsive web app/PWA instead of a native mobile application.

Non-goals

  • Marketplace functionality.
  • Customer accounts/passwords.
  • Integrated payment gateway in MVP.
  • Automatic supplier purchasing.
  • Complex warehouse management.
  • Automatic refunds in MVP.
  • Native iOS/Android apps.

3. Product Principles

  1. Batch-first: pre-orders are temporary campaigns rather than permanently open listings.
  2. Guest-first: customers should not need accounts.
  3. Manual where appropriate: payment verification and exceptional refund decisions remain admin-controlled.
  4. Inventory is explicit: physical stock, reserved quantity and surplus stock must be distinguishable.
  5. Backend is the source of truth: frontend actions cannot bypass business rules.
  6. Audit everything important: manual operations must be traceable.
  7. Mobile-first operations: staff should be able to run critical workflows from a phone.
  8. Do not overbuild: avoid marketplace-style complexity that does not support the actual business model.

5. Sales Models

5.1 Pre-Order

A pre-order product is available only while its batch is open. Customers place orders during that window. After closure, the business decides how to fulfil supplier MOQ and procurement requirements.

MOQ Example

Customer orders:      20
Supplier MOQ:         24
Procured:             24
Customer reserved:    20
Surplus ready stock:   4

The system must therefore distinguish customer-reserved quantity from unallocated/ready-stock quantity.

5.2 Ready Stock

Ready-stock products are sold from physical on-hand inventory. A product may transition from being part of a pre-order batch into ready stock when supplier quantity exceeds customer commitments.

6. End-to-End Business Flow

PRE-ORDER

Batch opens
→ Customer submits order + payment information
→ Payment pending
→ Admin verifies payment
→ Customer quantity reserved
→ Batch closes
→ Admin decides procurement quantity / MOQ
→ Supplier sends goods
→ Business receives stock
→ Customer order becomes fulfilment-ready
→ If DP: balance requested and verified
→ Pickup OR shipping
→ Completed

READY STOCK

Product has on-hand available quantity
→ Customer orders + submits payment
→ Admin verifies payment
→ Stock reserved
→ Pickup OR shipping
→ Completed

7. Customer Ordering

7.1 Guest Checkout

No customer account is required. The minimum identity/contact model is:

  • Customer name
  • Phone number
  • Email address
  • Fulfilment information

Phone number is the primary customer contact/lookup value. Secure order tokens can be delivered through the checkout success page and email.

7.2 Order + Payment Submission

CHANGED v1.3 Order creation and initial payment submission are one atomic flow. The customer does not create an order and then attach payment later.

The order is only persisted after:

  • Customer identity (name, phone, email)
  • Items/variants/quantities
  • Payment type (DP or FULL)
  • Payment proof / transfer reference (uploaded in the same form)
  • Fulfilment method

There is no "order created but payment pending submission" state. If the form is not completed, no order exists.

8. Payment

8.1 MVP

Payment is handled through bank transfer. The customer submits payment information/proof as part of the order flow. Admin manually checks the bank transfer and verifies or rejects it.

8.2 Payment Types

  • FULL: applicable merchandise/payment amount is paid upfront.
  • DP: customer pays a deposit and the remaining amount is paid after stock is ready.

DP is a single global rule — 50% of order total — applied system-wide, not configurable per batch or per product. Which payment type(s) are offered at checkout is controlled per batch (§10.1); the DP amount itself is not.

8.3 Verification

Every payment must have an explicit verification state and an admin actor.

DataDescription
AmountAmount submitted/verified.
Submitted atTimestamp.
StatusPending / Verified / Rejected.
Verified byAdmin user.
Verified atTimestamp.
Rejection reasonRequired when rejected.

Important: a submitted transfer must never automatically become a verified payment simply because the form was submitted.

Uploaded payment proof files are retained for 30 days after the related order reaches a completed fulfilment state (SHIPPED or PICKED_UP), then purged.

10.3 MOQ Decision

11. Inventory & Reservation

11.1 Definitions

  • On-hand: physical stock currently received by the business.
  • Reserved: physical quantity committed to confirmed customer orders.
  • Available: on-hand minus reserved quantity.
  • Incoming: quantity ordered from supplier but not physically received.
  • Surplus: received quantity remaining after customer commitments are allocated.
Available = On-hand − Reserved

11.2 Rules

  • Pre-order commitments are tracked even before physical stock exists.
  • When supplier stock is received, physical inventory is increased.
  • Customer commitments are allocated first.
  • Any excess quantity becomes available ready stock.
  • Inventory adjustments require a reason and audit event.
  • The system must prevent accidental negative available stock.

12. Ready Stock

Ready stock is inventory that can be sold immediately. Surplus from an MOQ procurement can be converted into ready stock after all eligible customer reservations are accounted for.

Admin should explicitly control whether newly created surplus is immediately published for sale or kept unpublished.

13. Fulfilment

13.1 Methods

  • Booth pickup
  • Shipping

13.2 Booth Pickup

Eligible customers receive a QR linked to their order. Staff scans it from a phone using the web/PWA camera.

The scan result should show:

  • Order ID
  • Customer name
  • Masked phone number
  • Items and quantities
  • Payment status
  • Pickup eligibility

Staff must explicitly confirm the pickup. The system then records the staff member and timestamp.

13.3 QR Behaviour

The QR should contain a random, unguessable token rather than sensitive customer information. The token is validated server-side.

It does not need to be physically destroyed after one scan. Instead, the backend makes the order state authoritative:

VALID QR + READY_FOR_PICKUP
→ show order
→ staff confirms pickup
→ PICKED_UP

Same QR after pickup
→ backend detects PICKED_UP
→ deny second fulfilment
→ show "Already picked up"

QR scanning is staff-authenticated only. If the scanning session is not an authenticated staff session, the app shows a generic "Login required" message and nothing else.

14. QR / Barcode Scanner

The scanner is a browser/PWA feature. There is no requirement for a native app.

  • Request camera permission when scanning starts.
  • Support modern mobile browsers.
  • Validate every scan against the backend.
  • Provide manual Order ID fallback.
  • Show clear success/error states.
  • Do not put sensitive customer information into the QR.

QR is recommended for customer pickup. Standard barcodes can additionally be supported later for internal product/inventory scanning.

16.1 Secure Order Access

CHANGED v1.3 The order URL contains a high-entropy random token (minimum 32 characters, URL-safe base64). It is not guessable.
  • The token is generated server-side and stored hashed in the database.
  • The token alone grants read-only access to the order page.
  • Rate limiting: The endpoint accepts maximum 10 requests per minute per IP for token-based access.
  • No expiration: The token does not expire, but can be revoked by admin if fraud is suspected.

16.2 Order Access Recovery

If a guest loses their link, they can recover access by:

  1. Entering their phone number on the lookup page.
  2. The system cross-checks the phone against the email address on file for that order.
  3. If both match, the existing order page (with the same token URL) is displayed.
  4. No new email, no new token, no SMS is sent. The customer simply re-opens the link they already had.

17. Email Notification Strategy

CHANGED v1.3 Email is transactional and minimal. Because the order page is the source of truth, emails are short nudges containing only the event type and a single link.

17.1 Core Email Events (4 emails only)

EventCustomer EmailPriorityContent

Skipped Events (v1.3)

  • Payment verified as standalone — merged into "Order confirmed"
  • Shipped — customer sees tracking on the order page
  • Picked up — customer was physically present

17.2 Email Body Rules

  • One link only. No order details, no tables, no images in the email body.
  • Subject line contains order ID and action keyword.
  • Footer: business name only. No unsubscribe needed (transactional).

17.3 P0 Queue-Jumping

P0 emails (payment rejected, balance due) jump ahead of P1 in the queue. If the queue is backed up, P1 emails wait; P0 emails send immediately.

19. Security

  • HTTPS everywhere.
  • Authenticated admin area.
  • Server-side authorization and role checks.
  • Secure session cookies.
  • Strong/random customer order access tokens.
  • Unpredictable QR tokens.
  • Rate limiting on public lookup and scan endpoints.
  • CSRF protection where applicable.
  • Input validation and output encoding.
  • Upload validation for payment proofs.
  • Idempotency/duplicate-submission protection for order creation, implemented via a one-time submission token issued with the order form plus a disabled-on-click submit button.
  • Do not expose bank/payment credentials unnecessarily in logs.
  • Audit security-sensitive actions.
  • Admin MFA should be supported where feasible.

24.2 Queue Worker Logic

Order event → Create email job (P0 or P1)
            → Queue
            → Worker checks daily counter
            → Resend API
            → Delivery status → Email log

Cap Rules

  • P0 (payment rejected, balance due): Send immediately. If Resend returns 429 (daily limit hit), queue for next day 00:01. Never dropped.
  • P1 (order confirmed, ready for fulfilment): Send only if today's count < 90. If cap hit, defer to next day.
  • Balance due batching: If >80 DP orders become balance-due simultaneously, send in batches of 80/day, ordered by payment verification time (earliest first). Remainder sends the next day.

24.3 Graceful Degradation

Critical business state never depends on email delivery. An order can become READY_FOR_FULFILMENT even if the email provider temporarily fails. The admin dashboard and customer order page remain the source of truth.

24.4 Future Upgrade Path

When revenue justifies it:

  1. Purchase custom domain (~$10/year)
  2. Verify domain in Resend
  3. Switch sender to noreply@yourstore.com
  4. Optionally upgrade to Resend paid ($20/mo, 50K emails, no daily cap)

25. PWA & Camera Architecture

The application should be a responsive web app enhanced with PWA capabilities. Staff can install it to their phone home screen and use the camera scanner without installing a native app.

Scanner Architecture

Staff opens PWA scanner
→ Browser requests camera permission
→ Camera detects QR
→ Frontend receives token
→ Backend validates token + order state
→ Order details returned
→ Staff confirms pickup
→ Backend changes order state
→ Audit event created

The PWA may cache static application assets, but transaction/order data must remain server-authoritative.

26. Edge Cases & Business Rules

  • Duplicate order submission must not create accidental duplicate orders.
  • Payment can be rejected and resubmitted according to configured rules.
  • DP orders remain balance-due until remaining payment is verified.
  • Supplier delivers fewer units than procured: orders are fulfilled in order of payment verification time (earliest-verified first); orders that cannot be fulfilled from the shortfall are cancelled manually by admin.
  • Supplier delivers more units: excess becomes ready stock.
  • Batch closes below MOQ: admin decides whether to extend or procure MOQ.
  • Customer cancellation is permitted any time before the order reaches SHIPPED (shipping orders) or PICKED_UP (pickup orders); refund decisions remain manual per business policy.
  • Duplicate order submission is prevented via a one-time submission token plus a disabled-on-click submit button — this only blocks accidental double-submits. A customer intentionally placing a second, separate order is expected and allowed.
  • Shipping cost can be manually corrected, but the override must be audited.
  • Courier API failure must not prevent manual shipment processing.
  • Email failure must not roll back order state.
  • Already-picked-up QR must never release goods a second time.
  • Invalid QR must produce a safe error without revealing customer information.
  • Inventory adjustments must not silently create negative stock.

27. Security & Privacy Detail

Order Link Security

CHANGED v1.3 Order access tokens are cryptographically random (32+ bytes, base64url encoded).
  • Tokens are stored hashed (SHA-256) in the database; the raw token only exists in the customer's URL.
  • The order page exposes no sensitive data to unauthenticated requests beyond what the token authorizes.
  • Brute-force protection: 10 failed token lookups per IP per minute triggers a temporary block.

QR Security

  • QR payload contains a random token only — no customer name, phone, or order details in the barcode itself.
  • Server validates token + order state before returning any data.
  • Scanning requires an authenticated staff session. Unauthenticated scans return only: "Login required."

Admin Security

  • Strong authentication.
  • Session timeout.
  • Server-side authorization.
  • MFA where feasible.
  • Audit logs that cannot be edited through the normal UI.

31. Key Architectural Decisions

  1. PWA instead of native: current staff use case does not justify maintaining iOS/Android apps.
  2. QR for pickup: flexible and suitable for secure order identification.
  3. Manual payment: avoids payment gateway cost and complexity initially.
  4. Shipping as a separate charge: keeps merchandise and logistics accounting clear.
  5. Email queue: allows delayed/batched sending and provider failover.
  6. No customer accounts: phone + secure order access is sufficient for the expected use.
  7. Audit log: critical because many operational decisions are manual.
  8. Workflow-driven model: batch/order lifecycle is more important than marketplace-style catalogue complexity.
  9. Resend free tier for MVP: $0 cost, no domain required, acceptable trade-offs for a small business starting out.
  10. Web push as backup: bypasses email caps and spam filters without requiring accounts or paid services.

32. Acceptance Criteria — Core Scenarios

Scenario A — Full-payment pre-order

  • Customer places guest order.
  • Initial payment is submitted in the same flow.
  • Order starts PAYMENT_PENDING.
  • Admin verifies payment.
  • System sends order confirmed email (P1).
  • Customer quantity becomes reserved.
  • Order waits for stock.

Scenario B — DP pre-order

  • Customer selects DP.
  • DP is submitted and manually verified.
  • Order remains reserved/awaiting stock.
  • Stock is received.
  • System calculates balance.
  • Customer receives balance-due notification (P0 email + push).
  • Remaining payment is manually verified.
  • Order becomes fulfilment-ready.

Scenario C — MOQ surplus

  • Admin sees customer quantity and MOQ.
  • Admin chooses to extend or procure MOQ.
  • Supplier quantity is recorded when received.
  • Customer commitments are allocated first.
  • Surplus becomes available ready stock.

Scenario D — Booth pickup

  • Eligible order has pickup QR.
  • Staff opens scanner on phone.
  • Camera scans QR.
  • Backend validates token and order state.
  • Staff sees order details.
  • Staff confirms pickup.
  • Order becomes PICKED_UP and audit log is written.
  • Repeated scan cannot cause another fulfilment.

Scenario E — Shipping

  • Customer selects shipping.
  • Address is collected.
  • JNE rate is obtained from configured shipping integration where available.
  • Shipping cost is stored separately.
  • Admin packs order.
  • Shipping sticker is generated.
  • Tracking number is recorded.
  • Order becomes SHIPPED.
  • Customer sees tracking on order page.

33. Open Decisions Before Implementation

  1. Exact DP percentage/amount. — Resolved v1.2: single global rule, 50% of order total.
  2. Exact moment balance becomes due after stock is ready. — Resolved v1.2: immediately once stock is received.
  3. Whether fulfilment method is selected at checkout or can be chosen later.
  4. Exact JNE API/provider and available service levels.
  5. Shipping label paper/printer format.
  6. Email provider and operational sending limit. — Resolved v1.3: Resend free tier, 100/day hard cap.
  7. How long unpaid/DP orders retain reservations. — Partially resolved v1.2: no automatic release — admin is notified via dashboard and follows up manually; no fixed timeout.
  8. Cancellation/refund policy. — Partially resolved v1.2: cancellation window is before SHIPPED/PICKED_UP; refund decisions remain manual per business policy.
  9. Whether batch extension automatically emails existing customers.
  10. Whether surplus stock is auto-published or requires admin approval.
  11. Exact admin role permissions for the three staff. — Resolved v1.2: per-action permission toggles, not fixed roles (§18.4).

34. Success Metrics

  • Reduced manual spreadsheet/order tracking.
  • Lower fulfilment error rate.
  • Time required to verify and fulfil an order.
  • Inventory discrepancy rate.
  • Successful pickup QR verification rate.
  • Email delivery success rate.
  • Percentage of orders with complete audit history.
  • Time from stock receipt to fulfilment readiness.

35. Final Product Definition

This is not a marketplace. It is a controlled sales, inventory and fulfilment system for a small business.

The customer experience stays simple: browse → order → pay → wait for stock if necessary → pickup or ship. The admin side handles the complexity of batches, MOQ, procurement, inventory allocation, payment verification and fulfilment.

The key domain concept is the distinction between customer commitments and physical inventory. When a batch requires MOQ procurement, customer reservations are fulfilled first; excess supplier quantity can become ready stock. This lets the same system support both pre-order and ready-stock sales without turning into a marketplace.

PWA is the recommended operational interface because staff can scan QR codes using their phone cameras directly from the browser, while the backend remains the authoritative source for order, payment, inventory and fulfilment state.
